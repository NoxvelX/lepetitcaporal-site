Orders travel

Every order is written, handed to a and carried across the battlefield on horseback. Distance, roads and the enemy decide how long it takes, and whether it arrives at all.

  1. Written and sealed at headquarters
  2. The courier rides out on the Maida road
  3. Through the olive grove, under fire
  4. Delivered. Now the general decides

Commanders think for themselves

Your subordinates read your order, weigh what they can see and act in their own way. A vague order can be misread; a clear one still leaves them room to decide how.

Questions and answers

What kind of game is this?

A real-time strategy game about command, not about clicking. You write orders, couriers carry them, and your generals carry them out as they understand them. You see the battle through reports, and reports are late.

Which battle does it begin with?

Maida, 4 July 1806, in Calabria. You take the place of General Reynier with the troops and the knowledge he had. There is also a drill battle at Kleinsdorf (winter 1807) and a tutorial for learning the formations.

What does it run on?

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, through Steam. The Steam page is not open yet.

Which languages does the game speak?

The game's texts are in English and Russian now. Ukrainian, Spanish, French, German, Polish and Italian are planned. Georgian is a language of this site only.

Is there multiplayer?

The game is single-player. Co-operative play is planned and is not in the game yet.

When does it come out, and what will it cost?

Neither a date nor a price has been announced.

How historical is it?

The field of Maida is built from maps of the place; the orders of battle and the drill come from sources of the period. Where the sources disagree a choice had to be made, and it may change as research goes on.

Words of the period

Press a dotted word anywhere on the page to read its note.

Dispatch
An order or a report on paper. While it is on the road the addressee goes on as before: he knows nothing yet.
Courier
A rider with a dispatch. Faster on a road than across fields; his horse tires. He can be killed or taken, and then the word is lost.
Aide-de-camp
A staff officer. A senior aide carries a complex order himself and explains it by word of mouth, which makes it clearer.
Report
A dispatch upwards: what a unit sees and does. You can ask for one: a courier rides to the subordinate, waits for the answer and brings it back.
Line
The battalion on its full frontage: the most muskets fire at once. Slow to move and open on the flank.
Attack column
A narrow, deep formation: quicker on the move and harder in a bayonet charge, but only its head can fire.
Square
The formation against cavalry: the battalion faces every way. Horsemen can hardly break it, but to guns and infantry it is a large target.
Skirmish order
Men spread out as marksmen: they screen their own and harass the enemy. Their fire is not commanded: each man fires by the book.
Battery
Guns with their crews, limbers and caissons. It fires only in position; its captain chooses the target and the charge.
Brigade
Regiments under one general. Your order to a brigade is carried out by its general, who writes his own orders to his regiments.
Battalion
The infantry unit that forms and fires as one. Its chef de bataillon commands it; his colonel gives him his orders.
